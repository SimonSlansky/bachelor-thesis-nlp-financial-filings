\section{Descriptive statistics}
\label{sec:descriptive}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:summary} summarises the 30-day estimation sample of 5{,}782
firm-years. The median annualised 30-day post-filing volatility is 0.243
(mean 0.317). The median Item~1A has 8{,}700 tokens, with a long right tail
(99th percentile 35{,}579). The mean RiskDensity is 0.0511, so about one
token in twenty is an uncertainty or litigious word; the uncertainty
sub-list (mean density 0.0346) is about twice as dense as the litigious one
(0.0165).

\input{tables/summary_statistics}

Figure~\ref{fig:vol_time} plots the median 30-day post-filing volatility by
fiscal year with the inter-quartile range. Volatility peaks for fiscal year
2019, whose 10-Ks were filed in early 2020, just before and during the
market turmoil of spring 2020. The fiscal-year fixed effects absorb such
common shocks to the extent that they hit all filings of a fiscal year.

Table~\ref{tab:correlation} reports pairwise correlations. Length and
RiskDensity are negatively correlated ($r = -0.30$): longer sections have a
lower share of dictionary risk words, mainly uncertainty words ($r = -0.44$).
The two sub-densities are almost uncorrelated ($r = -0.07$). Pre-filing and
post-filing volatility have a correlation of 0.53, which is why pre-filing
volatility is the key control.

\section{Length and risk-word density (H1 and H2)}
\label{sec:joint_results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:main_30d} reports five nested specifications on the same
sample: the controls only (1), length (2), density (3), length and density
jointly (4), and the sub-list decomposition (5).

\input{tables/main_30d}

\paragraph{H1.} In the joint specification (column 4) a longer Item~1A is
associated with higher post-filing volatility: $\hat\beta^{\text{len}} =
+0.053$ ($t = +4.51$). The coefficient is similar in columns 2 and 5 and
remains significant with a wild cluster bootstrap by year ($p = 0.001$;
Table~\ref{tab:inference}). \textbf{H1 is supported} in the industry
fixed-effects design. Section~\ref{sec:robustness_firmfe} shows that the
association does not hold within firms over time.

\paragraph{H2.} Holding length fixed, the composite RiskDensity has a negative
coefficient ($\hat\beta^{\text{dens}} = -1.845$, $t = -1.70$, $p = 0.090$;
wild bootstrap $p = 0.092$). Without the length control (column 3) it is
$-3.408$ ($t = -3.33$): length and density are negatively correlated, so part
of the density coefficient in column 3 reflects length. Because H2 is
two-sided, the composite test does not reject $\beta^{\text{dens}} = 0$ at
the 5\% level: \textbf{H2 is not supported at the 30-day horizon}. At longer
horizons the composite coefficient is negative and significant at the 5\%
level from 90 days on ($t = -3.33$ at 365 days; Table~\ref{tab:main_365d}).

\paragraph{Sub-lists.} In column 5 the uncertainty density has no association
with volatility ($\hat\beta^{\text{unc}} = -0.545$, $t = -0.31$), and the
litigious density a negative one ($\hat\beta^{\text{lit}} = -3.018$,
$t = -2.90$; wild bootstrap $p = 0.005$). The Wald test does not reject that
the two coefficients are equal ($p = 0.179$), so the data do not establish
that the two sub-lists behave differently; the negative sign of the composite
comes mainly from the litigious words. The negative sign is in line with the
boilerplate channel of Section~\ref{sec:h2} rather than the risk-content
channel, but the design cannot tell which feature of litigious language
drives it.

\paragraph{Magnitudes.} Table~\ref{tab:effects} expresses the coefficients on
a common basis. An increase of one pooled standard deviation in
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ (0.65 log points) is associated with 3.8\% higher
30-day volatility, and an increase of one pooled standard deviation in
LitDensity with 1.4\% lower volatility; for the composite RiskDensity the
figure is $-1.2\%$. Variation within a firm is much smaller (one
within-firm standard deviation of length is 0.27 log points), so the same
coefficients imply 1.6\% and $-0.6\%$ for within-firm changes. At the median
volatility of 0.243 a 3.8\% change is about 0.9 percentage points of
annualised volatility. The text variables add little explained variance:
the within-$R^2$ rises from 0.363 with the controls alone to 0.368
(Table~\ref{tab:main_30d}).

\input{tables/effect_sizes}

\paragraph{365-day horizon.} Table~\ref{tab:main_365d} repeats the five
columns with the 365-day window on the same firm-years. Length remains
positive ($+0.084$, $t = +8.64$ in column 5), LitDensity negative
($-4.709$, $t = -5.38$) and UncDensity insignificant; the Wald test rejects
equality of the two sub-list coefficients at this horizon ($p < 0.001$).

\section{The omission gap (H3)}
\label{sec:silence_results}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:silence_main} reports the H3 specification at six horizons
under both fixed-effect structures, for the omission gap and for its
length-invariant version.

\input{tables/silence_main}

At the 30-day horizon the omission coefficient is $+0.081$ ($t = +0.37$)
with industry fixed effects and $+0.475$ ($t = +0.70$) with firm fixed
effects; no coefficient in the family of ten tests is significant even
before the Holm adjustment, and all Holm-adjusted $p$-values equal one. The
length-invariant gap gives the same picture ($+0.487$, $t = +1.60$, and
$+0.351$, $t = +0.58$). Under the decision rule of Section~\ref{sec:h3},
\textbf{H3 is not supported}: the omission gap is not associated with
post-filing volatility once length, density and the controls are held fixed.
Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness} shows that no variant of the measure changes this
conclusion.
