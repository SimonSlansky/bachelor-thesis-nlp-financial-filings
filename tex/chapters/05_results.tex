\section{Descriptive statistics}
\label{sec:descriptive}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:summary} reports summary statistics for the 30-day
regression sample. The dependent variable has mean
$\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[30]} \approx -1.34$, corresponding to a
typical annualised post-filing volatility around $0.26$. Median
Item~1A length is $\exp(9.063) \approx 8{,}600$~words.
The mean RiskDensity of $0.051$ implies that about one in every 20
words in Item~1A is a risk word.
The LM-uncertainty sub-list is about twice as dense as the
LM-litigious sub-list.

\input{tables/summary_statistics}

Figure~\ref{fig:vol_time} plots the median annualised 30-day
post-filing volatility by fiscal year, together with the
inter-quartile band. Volatility spikes in 2020 and is otherwise
fairly stable. The fiscal-year fixed effects remove these common
shocks, and they motivate the pre-COVID / COVID-era split in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_subperiods}.

Table~\ref{tab:correlation} reports pairwise correlations. The two
text variables are correlated at $r = -0.30$: longer Item~1A
sections tend to have lower risk-word density, consistent with
length being driven by firm-specific text rather than by extra
generic risk vocabulary. The correlation is low enough for both
variables to be separately identified in the joint specification.

\section{Volume, specificity, and the LM-list decomposition}
\label{sec:joint_results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:main_30d} reports five nested specifications at the
30-day horizon: financial baseline (1), length only (2), density
only (3), the joint H1+H2 specification (4), and the LM-list
decomposition (5).

\input{tables/main_30d}

In the joint specification (Column~4), longer Item~1A sections
predict higher post-filing volatility ($\hat{\beta}^{\text{len}}
= +0.116$, $t = +7.26$) and, holding length fixed, denser sections
predict lower volatility ($\hat{\beta}^{\text{risk}} = -4.36$,
$t = -3.17$). The density coefficient roughly halves between Columns~3
and~4 (from $-8.09$ to $-4.36$): about half of the apparent ``risk-density effect'' in the
dictionary-only specification is the volume effect working through
the word-count denominator. \textbf{H1 and H2 are both
supported.}

To see how big these effects are, note that the dependent variable
is in logs, so a coefficient $\beta$ and a change $\Delta x$ in the
regressor give a change of about $100\,(e^{\beta\Delta x}-1)\%$ in
volatility. For length, one standard deviation ($0.653$ log points,
roughly a $90\%$ longer section) raises volatility by about $8\%$.
A smaller $10\%$ increase in length ($0.095$ log points) raises it
by about $1.1\%$. At the typical volatility of $0.26$, the
one-SD effect is about $2$~percentage points of annualised
volatility. For density, a one-SD rise in RiskDensity ($0.007$)
lowers volatility by about $3.0\%$. In the decomposition
(Column~5), a one-SD rise in LitDensity ($0.005$) lowers it by
about $3.2\%$. The length effect is the larger of the two; the
density effects are smaller.

When the composite RiskDensity is replaced by its two LM
sub-densities (Column~5), the negative association comes almost
entirely from the LM-Litigious sub-list:
$\hat{\beta}^{\text{lit}} = -6.55$ ($t = -3.83$, $p < 0.001$),
while $\hat{\beta}^{\text{unc}} = -1.88$ is not statistically
significant. A Wald test of equality of the two coefficients gives
$z = +1.86$ ($p = 0.063$). So the two coefficients are different
only at the $10\%$~level, not at the $5\%$~level, and the claim
that the two sub-lists behave differently should be read with care. The pattern is
consistent with \textcite{cazier2021,beatty2019}: litigious-list
language is standardised cautionary boilerplate, and once separated
from the uncertainty sub-list it accounts for most of the H2
result. The H2 result is a between-firm pattern: firms that
\emph{systematically} use more litigious vocabulary have lower
subsequent volatility. It does not mean that a firm which
\emph{increases} its litigious language has lower volatility,
because both sub-densities lose significance under firm fixed
effects (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_firmfe}).
Adjusted~$R^2$ rises only slightly across the five columns
($0.498 \to 0.513$) because the financial baseline already
explains most of the predictable volatility. Table~\ref{tab:main_365d}
reproduces the same five columns with the dependent variable
replaced by $\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[365]}$. The signs are unchanged
and the magnitudes are about half of the 30-day estimates.

\section{Strategic-silence hypothesis and within-firm post-filing volatility}
\label{sec:silence_results}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:silence_main} reports the H3 specification of
equation~\ref{eq:silence_model} at five post-filing horizons under
two fixed-effect structures: industry plus year FE in Panel~A and
firm plus year FE in Panel~B. The coefficient of interest is
$\beta^{\text{om}}$ on the peer-relative omission gap; Item~1A
length, RiskDensity, the five financial controls, and lagged
log-volatility are partialled out.

\input{tables/silence_main}

The cross-section in Panel~A is not significant at any horizon
($|t| < 1.3$). In the firm-FE specification of Panel~B the
coefficient is positive and significant at the 30-day horizon
($\hat{\beta}^{\text{om}} = +1.247$, $t = +2.58$, $p = 0.010$)
and also at 90 and 180~days. It is significant at the $5\%$~level
at 5~days and only at the $10\%$~level at 10~days. A one-within-firm-SD rise in
$\textsc{Omission}^{1A}$ ($0.043$) implies a $\approx 5.5\%$
increase in 30-day annualised volatility, close to the size of the
H1 length effect. One interpretation is that persistent firm-level
disclosure styles absorb the cross-sectional variation, while the
peer-relative omission gap, which by construction varies with
what \emph{other} firms write each year, remains after firm fixed
effects. Because the pre-specified criterion in
Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} required a positive $\beta^{\text{om}}$
under \emph{both} structures, and Panel~A is positive
($+0.238$) but not different from zero
($t = +0.65$), \textbf{H3 receives only partial support: it is
supported within-firm at the 30-day horizon but not in the
industry-FE cross-section}. Section~\ref{sec:robustness_silence}
shows that the within-firm result is also weaker in some
robustness checks.
