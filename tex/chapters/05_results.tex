\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}
% =============================================================================

This chapter reports the empirical results. Section~\ref{sec:descriptive}
summarises the regression sample. Section~\ref{sec:joint_results}
presents the headline test of H1 and H2 at the 30-day horizon and
introduces the LM-list decomposition column (the second-stage test
of H2).
Section~\ref{sec:annual_results} reproduces the same specification
at the 365-day horizon to confirm that the results are not an
artefact of the short-window choice.
Section~\ref{sec:horizons_results} runs the joint H1+H2 decomposition at
every horizon between 5 and 365~days, and
Section~\ref{sec:boilerplate_results} discusses the LM-list
decomposition evidence formally. Section~\ref{sec:silence_results}
introduces the strategic-silence specification (H3) and reports the
headline within-firm test, with full robustness deferred to
Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness}.

\section{Descriptive statistics}
\label{sec:descriptive}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:summary} reports summary statistics for the
regression sample at the 30-day horizon. The dependent variable
has mean $\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[30]} \approx -1.34$
(SD~$\approx 0.59$), corresponding to a typical annualised
post-filing volatility around $0.26$. Firm size centres on
$\$11.7$~billion in total assets, confirming the focus on large,
liquid firms. The two text variables show economically meaningful
spread: $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ has a standard deviation of
$0.64$ (median Item~1A $\approx 8{,}800$~words), and the LM
uncertainty and litigious sub-shares are essentially uncorrelated
($r \approx -0.05$), confirming that the H2 decomposition
delivers two genuinely separate regressors.

\input{tables/summary_statistics}

Table~\ref{tab:correlation} reports pairwise Pearson correlations.
Lagged log-volatility is the strongest single predictor of
log-volatility ($r = 0.35$). Larger firms have lower log-volatility
($r \approx -0.19$) and higher profitability also tracks lower
log-volatility ($r \approx -0.23$), in line with the well-known
size--risk and earnings-quality relationships. The two text
variables are correlated at $r = -0.30$: longer Item~1A sections
tend to have lower risk-word density, consistent with their
narrative being padded by firm-specific text rather than by extra
generic risk vocabulary. The negative correlation is moderate and
leaves both signals separately identifiable in the joint
specification.

\section{Volume, specificity, and boilerplate at the 30-day horizon}
\label{sec:joint_results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:main_30d} reports the headline test of H1 and H2,
together with the LM-list decomposition column. Column~(1) is the financial baseline; Column~(2) adds Item~1A
length in logs (the volume channel); Column~(3) adds the composite
risk-word density (the specificity channel); Column~(4) is the joint
specification of equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} that nests H1 and H2;
Column~(5) is the LM-list decomposition specification of
equation~\ref{eq:decomposition_model}, which replaces RiskDensity by
its two LM sub-components.

\input{tables/main_30d}

\paragraph{The baseline (Column~1).} Lagged log-volatility, firm
size (negative), ROA (negative), and asset growth (positive)
enter with the expected signs and conventional significance;
leverage is statistically indistinguishable from zero, as in much
of the volatility literature \parencite{campbell2014}. The
baseline already explains $49\%$ of the in-sample variation.

\paragraph{The volume channel (Columns~2, 4, and~5).} Adding
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ alone (Column~2) raises adjusted~$R^2$ to
$0.505$ and yields a length coefficient of $+0.138$ ($t = +8.43$).
In the joint specification (Column~4), where the composite
risk-word density is controlled for, the length coefficient is
$+0.121$ ($t = +7.64$). In the LM-list decomposition specification
(Column~5), where the composite is replaced by its two LM
sub-components, the length coefficient is $+0.130$
($t = +7.16$). The pattern is consistent across specifications:
longer risk-factor sections are associated with higher post-filing
log-volatility at the 30-day horizon. The point estimate of
$0.130$ from Column~(5) corresponds to roughly an $8\%$ increase
in post-filing volatility for a one-standard-deviation rise in
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ ($0.64 \times 0.130 = 0.083$, applied to
$\ln \sigma$). \textbf{H1 is supported.}

\paragraph{The specificity channel (Columns~3 and~4).} Adding
RiskDensity alone (Column~3) yields a coefficient of $-7.78$
($t = -5.40$). In the joint specification, where the volume channel
is controlled for, the coefficient remains negative and significant
at $-3.88$ ($t = -2.68$, $p = 0.007$). The fact that the density
coefficient halves once length is controlled for is exactly the
mechanical pattern anticipated in
Section~\ref{ssec:textual_features}: about half of what looked like a
``risk-density effect'' in Column~(3) was in fact the volume effect
operating through the section's word-count denominator. Once
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ is partialled out, the residual density
effect is purely specificity in the \textcite{hope2016} sense: a
denser, more boilerplate Item~1A is associated with \emph{lower}
post-filing volatility. \textbf{H2 is supported}, and the joint
specification reveals an effect that the dictionary-only ratio used
in much prior work would have hidden.

\paragraph{The boilerplate decomposition (Column~5).} Replacing
the composite RiskDensity by its two LM sub-components reveals
that the negative specificity association of Column~(4) is carried
almost entirely by the LM-Litigious sub-list. LitDensity enters
with a coefficient of $-6.16$ ($t = -3.38$, $p < 0.001$), while
UncDensity enters with a small and statistically insignificant
coefficient of $-1.28$ ($t = -0.62$, $p = 0.53$). A formal Wald
test of $H_0: \beta^{\text{unc}} = \beta^{\text{lit}}$ yields
$z = +1.91$ ($p = 0.056$), so the two coefficients are
distinguishable at the $10\%$~but not the $5\%$~level under the
full two-way clustering scheme. The $95\%$~confidence interval on
$\beta^{\text{unc}}$ is wide ($[-5.30, +2.74]$): the data are
consistent with a modestly negative uncertainty effect, but cannot
rule out zero. Adjusted $R^2$ in Column~(5) is essentially
identical to that of the composite specification in Column~(4)
($0.5068$ vs.\ $0.5064$), because the litigious sub-list captures
the variance that drives the composite. The H2 specificity
association is therefore concentrated in the litigious rather
than the uncertainty sub-list, consistent with the cautionary,
legal-process language that
\textcite{nelson2007,cazier2021} link to mature, legally
supervised disclosure environments. \textbf{The LM-list decomposition is supported in the
cross-section}, with the qualifications discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:boilerplate_results} and the firm-FE caveat of
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_firmfe}.

\paragraph{Joint significance and incremental fit.} The joint test
of $H_0: \beta^{\text{len}} = \beta^{\text{unc}} = \beta^{\text{lit}}
= 0$ in Column~(5) rejects at the 1\%~level. Adding the
three text variables to the baseline raises adjusted $R^2$ from
$0.492$ (Column~1) to $0.507$ (Column~5), an increment that is
small in absolute terms because the financial baseline already
captures the bulk of predictable volatility but is large by the
standards of the textual-disclosure literature
\parencite{kravet2013, campbell2014}. The three text variables are
informative both individually and jointly.

\section{The same channels at the 365-day horizon and across
intermediate windows}
\label{sec:annual_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_365d} re-estimates the same five nested
specifications with the dependent variable replaced by
$\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[365]}$. The signs and significance of all
three text variables are preserved: in the H1+H2 decomposition
(Column~5) $\beta^{\text{len}} = +0.061$ ($t = +4.93$),
$\beta^{\text{lit}} = -3.39$ ($t = -3.91$), and
$\beta^{\text{unc}}$ remains null. Magnitudes shrink to roughly
half of the 30-day estimates, consistent with the post-filing
reaction documented by \textcite{loughran2011}.

\section{Multi-horizon table}
\label{sec:horizons_results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:horizons} reports the H1+H2 decomposition
specification of equation~\ref{eq:decomposition_model} at six
post-filing horizons: $h \in \{5, 10, 30, 90, 180, 365\}$ calendar
days. Each column is a separate regression on the full panel;
financial controls and fixed effects are unchanged.

\input{tables/horizons}

Three patterns emerge. First, $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ enters with
a positive and highly significant coefficient at every horizon,
ranging from $+0.195$ at 5~days to $+0.061$ at 365~days, with
$t$-statistics between $+4.93$ and $+8.78$. The volume association is
robust across horizons. Second, LitDensity enters with a negative
coefficient at every horizon and is significant at the 1\% level
from 10~days onward; the $t$-statistics range from $-2.88$ at
10~days to $-4.02$ at 180~days. Third, UncDensity is small in
absolute value and statistically indistinguishable from zero at
every horizon, with $|t| \leq 1.1$ at all windows. The qualitative
pattern of Column~(5) of Table~\ref{tab:main_30d} therefore
replicates at every horizon the data admit: the negative
association between risk-word language and post-filing volatility
is a litigious-language association, not an uncertainty-language
one.

The lagged-dependent-variable coefficient also rises with horizon,
from about $0.030$ at 5~days to $0.566$ at 365~days, reflecting the
mechanical fact that longer windows for both the dependent and
the lagged variable share more of their underlying daily-return
sample. This is a feature of the data, not of the specification.

\section{The boilerplate decomposition}
\label{sec:boilerplate_results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The negative association of the composite RiskDensity with
post-filing volatility is driven entirely by the LM-Litigious
sub-list; once the litigious component is partialled out, the
LM-Uncertainty component has no incremental association at any
horizon. A one-standard-deviation rise in LitDensity
($\approx 0.005$) corresponds to a
$0.005 \times 6.16 \approx 2.8\%$ decrease in 30-day annualised
volatility, and the interquartile swing is $\approx 3.8\%$.
The reading aligns with the
\textcite{nelson2007,cazier2021,beatty2019} interpretation of
litigious-list language as standardised cautionary boilerplate
that proxies for an institutionalised, legally supervised
compliance function. The within-firm caveat is reserved for
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_firmfe}.

\section{Strategic silence and within-firm post-filing volatility}
\label{sec:silence_results}
% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:silence_main} reports the H3 strategic-silence
specification of equation~\ref{eq:silence_model} at five
post-filing horizons under both fixed-effect structures: industry
plus year FE in Panel~A and firm plus year FE in Panel~B. The
coefficient of interest is $\beta^{\text{om}}$ on the peer-relative
omission gap of equation~\ref{eq:omission}. Item~1A length is in
the regression as a control alongside RiskDensity, the five
financial controls, and lagged log-volatility, so $\beta^{\text{om}}$
measures the incremental association of peer-relative omission with
post-filing volatility holding the firm's own length and
risk-vocabulary density fixed.

\input{tables/silence_main}

\paragraph{Cross-section is null.} Panel~A of
Table~\ref{tab:silence_main} shows that, conditional on the H1+H2
controls and SIC2-and-year fixed effects, peer-relative omission
has no detectable cross-sectional association with post-filing
volatility at any horizon: $|t| < 1.1$ at every window. The
cross-sectional version of equation~\ref{eq:silence_model} does not
identify a between-firm strategic-silence effect on this panel.

\paragraph{Within-firm survival and economic magnitude.} Panel~B
reports the strict version with industry fixed effects replaced
by firm fixed effects. The omission coefficient is positive and
significant at the 30-day horizon
($\hat{\beta}^{\text{om}} = +1.345$, $t = +2.93$, $p = 0.003$),
and remains significant at 90 and 180~days ($t = +2.42$ and
$+2.35$); at 5 and 10~days the coefficient is positive but
insignificant. A one-within-firm-SD rise in $\textsc{Omission}^{1A}$
($0.054$) implies a $\approx 7.3\%$ increase in 30-day annualised
volatility, of the same order as the cross-sectional H1 length
effect. \textbf{H3 is supported within-firm at the 30-day
horizon}, with the qualifications discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_silence}.

\paragraph{Cross-section vs.\ within-firm.} The H3 omission gap
shows the opposite pattern to the H2 LM sub-densities: no
cross-sectional power but a within-firm signal. The reading is
that permanent firm-level disclosure styles absorb the
cross-section, whereas the omission gap, by construction, varies
with what \emph{other} firms write each year and is therefore a
year-specific peer-relative signal that the firm-FE
specification lets through. An alternative reading---omission
proxies for a year-specific firm shock that drives both shorter
Item~1A and higher subsequent volatility---cannot be ruled out
from these regressions alone; the placebo, length-residualised,
and lead-lag checks of Section~\ref{sec:robustness_silence} are
designed to discriminate between the two.
