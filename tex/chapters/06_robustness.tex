\section{Alternative post-filing windows}
\label{sec:robustness_horizons}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The main specifications use the annualised standard deviation of
daily log returns over a 365-calendar-day window starting two trading
days after the filing date.  Choosing one year matches the annual
frequency of the panel, but it has two costs that bear directly on
hypothesis testing.  First, the window is long enough that the next
10-K is filed before it closes, so the back half of the window
overlaps with information from the subsequent disclosure.  Second,
the prior literature on textual signals in 10-Ks consistently finds
that any incremental predictive power is concentrated in the first
few weeks or months after the filing and decays thereafter
\parencite{loughran2011}.  An annual measure averages this
short-lived signal against several months of unrelated noise.

To assess the robustness of the main results to this choice, the
divergence specification (equation~\ref{eq:divergence_model}) is
re-estimated with the dependent variable replaced by the annualised
volatility computed over windows of 30, 90, and 180~calendar days
following the filing.  The lagged dependent variable is correspondingly
the same firm's volatility measured over the prior filing year's
window of the same length.  All other regressors, fixed effects, and
the firm-clustered standard-error structure are unchanged.
Table~\ref{tab:horizons} reports the four horizons side by side; the
365-day column reproduces the headline divergence model from
Chapter~\ref{ch:results} and serves as the long-horizon benchmark.
