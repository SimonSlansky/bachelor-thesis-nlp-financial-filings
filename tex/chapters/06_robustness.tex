\section{Alternative horizons}
\label{sec:robustness_horizons}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:horizons} re-estimates the composite and decomposition
specifications with volatility over 5, 10, 30, 90, 180 and 365 days. The
length coefficient is positive and significant at the 1\% level at every
horizon (between $+0.058$ and $+0.101$ in the decomposition). LitDensity is
negative and significant from 30 days on ($t$ between $-2.90$ and $-5.38$),
but not at 5 or 10 days, where the windows contain few returns. UncDensity
is never significant. The composite RiskDensity is significant at the 5\%
level from 90 days on. Figure~\ref{fig:horizon} plots the length and
LitDensity coefficients. The coefficients are not compared formally across
horizons, because the windows overlap and the estimates are correlated.

\section{Robustness of the H1 and H2 estimates}
\label{sec:robustness_h12}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:robust_30d} reports the decomposition specification under
alternative choices, one change per row (Table~\ref{tab:robust_365d} in
Appendix~\ref{app:variables} repeats it at 365 days).

\input{tables/robustness_30d}

Length remains positive in the alternative samples, timing rules and industry 
definitions. The count-based model is an important exception. In the main model 
a longer section at fixed density contains proportionally more dictionary words. 
In the count model a longer section at fixed dictionary counts contains more other 
words. Its length coefficient is $-0.050$ ($t=-1.76$), and the sample rises from 
5{,}782 to 5{,}855. Re-estimating the count model on the original 5{,}782 observations 
gives a positive length coefficient ($+0.115$, $t=2.00$). The sign reversal in the larger 
sample therefore cannot be attributed to the choice of counts alone. Litigious density 
is sensitive to sample selection: excluding imputed balance-sheet observations reduces 
its coefficient from $-3.018$ to $-1.761$ ($t=-1.05$).

\section{Firm fixed effects}
\label{sec:robustness_firmfe}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The headline models adjust for industry and fiscal year. Replacing the
industry effects by firm effects asks a different question: whether a firm
whose Item~1A becomes longer or denser than usual has higher volatility
than usual. Table~\ref{tab:firm_fe} reports the answer, and
Figure~\ref{fig:coef_fe} compares the coefficients.

\input{tables/firm_fe}

Under firm fixed effects the length coefficient falls from $+0.058$ to
$+0.009$ ($t = +0.45$), and no density coefficient is significant
(LitDensity $-3.132$, $t = -1.02$). The litigious-density estimate is similar 
in magnitude under the two designs, but much less precise with firm effects. 
Only 18\% of the variation in log length and 19\% of the variation in litigious 
density occurs within firms (Table~\ref{tab:variance}). The data therefore give 
clearer evidence for differences across firms than for a relationship within the 
same firm. Column 3 adds last year's post-filing outcome. With short firm histories, 
subtracting each firm's average makes this lag partly correlated with the remaining error, 
which can bias its coefficient \parencite{nickell1981}. The main comparison uses 
columns 1 and 2, which control for volatility measured before the current filing.

\section{The omission measure (H3)}
\label{sec:robustness_silence}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

H3 is not supported in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. The main measurement limitation 
is the relationship with section length. Additional checks appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_robust}.

\paragraph{Length.} The omission gap is strongly related to length:
$r = -0.915$ with $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ in the H3 sample of 4{,}659
firm-years (Figure~\ref{fig:omission_length}), indicating that the measure
is closely tied to disclosure length.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{img/fig_omission_length.pdf}
\caption{Omission gap against $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$, one point per
firm-year of the 30-day H3 sample, with the OLS fit.}
\label{fig:omission_length}
\end{figure}

The 3{,}000-token block check weakens this relationship and yields no 
significant coefficient at the primary 30-day horizon under either fixed-effect 
structure. Because the two measures still depend on vocabulary and available peers, 
the results leave open whether firms omit economically important risks.
