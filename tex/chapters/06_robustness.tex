\section{Alternative horizons}
\label{sec:robustness_horizons}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:horizons} re-estimates the composite and decomposition
specifications with volatility over 5, 10, 30, 90, 180 and 365 days. The
length coefficient is positive and significant at the 1\% level at every
horizon (between $+0.058$ and $+0.101$ in the decomposition). LitDensity is
negative and significant from 30 days on ($t$ between $-2.90$ and $-5.38$),
but not at 5 or 10 days, where the windows contain few returns. UncDensity
is never significant. The composite RiskDensity is significant at the 5\%
level from 90 days on. Figure~\ref{fig:horizon} plots the length and
LitDensity coefficients. The coefficients are not compared formally across
horizons, because the windows overlap and the estimates are correlated.

\input{tables/robustness_30d}

The length coefficient stays positive when I compare firms that filed in the same calendar month and when I exclude imputed balance-sheet values. Excluding those values weakens the legal-word coefficient from $-3.018$ to $-1.761$ ($t=-1.05$).

Using word counts changes the comparison. At a fixed dictionary share, a longer section contains more dictionary words. At a fixed dictionary count, the extra words come from outside the dictionaries. The count model also admits 73 additional filings because counts are available for some short or poorly extracted sections that have no measured density. Its length coefficient is $-0.050$ ($t=-1.76$) across all 5{,}855 observations and $+0.115$ ($t=2.00$) on the original 5{,}782. The change in sign therefore depends on which filings enter the comparison.

\section{Firm fixed effects}
\label{sec:robustness_firmfe}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The headline models adjust for industry and fiscal year. Replacing the
industry effects by firm effects asks a different question: whether a firm
whose Item~1A becomes longer or denser than usual has higher volatility
than usual. Table~\ref{tab:firm_fe} reports the answer, and
Figure~\ref{fig:coef_fe} compares the coefficients.

\input{tables/firm_fe}

Under firm fixed effects the length coefficient falls from $+0.058$ to
$+0.009$ ($t = +0.45$), and no density coefficient is significant
(LitDensity $-3.132$, $t = -1.02$). The litigious-density estimate is similar 
in magnitude under the two designs, but much less precise with firm effects. 
Only 18\% of the variation in log length and 19\% of the variation in litigious 
density occurs within firms (Table~\ref{tab:variance}). The data therefore give 
clearer evidence for differences across firms than for a relationship within the 
same firm. Disclosure practices can change slowly, and the short firm histories make long-run patterns difficult to assess.

\section{The omission measure (H3)}
\label{sec:robustness_silence}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Figure~\ref{fig:omission_length} shows why section length matters for H3. Longer sections cover more of their peers' vocabulary. Across the 4{,}659 firm-years in the H3 sample, the full-text gap has a correlation of $-0.915$ with log section length.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{img/fig_omission_length.pdf}
\caption{Omission gap against $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$, one point per
firm-year of the 30-day H3 sample, with the OLS fit.}
\label{fig:omission_length}
\end{figure}

Using passages of a common target length weakens the correlation with log section length and reverses its sign ($r=+0.424$). At 30 days the block-based gap has no statistically significant association with volatility in either model. A missing word may simply mean that the firm described the same risk differently. Appendix~\ref{sec:app_robust} reports the estimates.
