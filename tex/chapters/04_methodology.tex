\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}
% =============================================================================

This chapter explains how the data described in
Chapter~\ref{ch:data} are used to test the three hypotheses set out
in Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}. Section~\ref{sec:design} gives an
overview of the empirical strategy. Section~\ref{sec:variables}
defines the regression variables and Section~\ref{sec:models}
formalises the joint specification used for H1 and H2 and the
LM-list-decomposition specification used for H3.
Section~\ref{sec:estimation} describes estimation and inference,
and Section~\ref{sec:hypothesis_tests} states the formal decision
rules used to evaluate each hypothesis.
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_plan} previews the robustness checks
reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness}.


% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Empirical strategy}
\label{sec:design}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The thesis tests two competing predictions about the same section
of the 10-K---Item~1A, the mandatory risk-factor disclosure---in a
single regression specification. The volume channel
\parencite{campbell2014} predicts that longer risk-factor sections
indicate more risk and therefore higher post-filing volatility. The
specificity channel \parencite{hope2016} predicts that, holding
length constant, a higher share of \emph{generic} risk vocabulary
indicates a more boilerplate and less informative section, and is
therefore associated with \emph{lower} post-filing volatility.
Because the dictionary risk-density commonly used in prior work has
section length in its denominator, separating the two channels
requires entering log word count and risk-word density jointly.

Once the joint specification is in place, H3 looks inside the
composite RiskDensity. The same headline regression is re-estimated
with the LM uncertainty- and litigious-list densities entered
separately as $\text{UncDensity}$ and $\text{LitDensity}$, and the
two coefficients are inspected for which sub-list is the active
ingredient of the H2 effect. By construction
$\text{RiskDensity} = \text{UncDensity} + \text{LitDensity}$, so the
decomposition is an algebraic refinement of H2, not a competing
specification. The same six post-filing horizons
($h \in \{5, 10, 30, 90, 180, 365\}$~days) are used in the
robustness chapter to confirm that the sub-list that is identified as
active at the headline horizon retains its sign and significance
across all alternative windows.

The dependent variable is the natural log of post-filing annualised
volatility. The log transformation has two advantages. First, raw
volatility is bounded below by zero and right-skewed, so OLS in
levels gives disproportionate weight to the right tail; the log
transformation symmetrises the distribution and makes coefficients
on continuous regressors directly interpretable as
semi-elasticities. Second, the log transformation makes the
specification scale-free: a one-unit change in
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ corresponds to a constant percentage change
in volatility regardless of the firm's baseline level of risk.

\section{Variables}
\label{sec:variables}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Dependent variable: post-filing log-volatility}
\label{ssec:dependent_variable}

The dependent variable is the natural logarithm of the annualised
standard deviation of daily log returns computed over a post-filing
window of length~$h$ that begins two trading days after the 10-K
filing date:
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \ln\!\left(
\sqrt{252}\cdot
\operatorname{sd}\!\bigl(r_{i,d}\bigr)_{d \in W_{i,t}^{[h]}}
\right),
\label{eq:vol}
\end{equation}
where $r_{i,d} = \ln(P_{i,d}/P_{i,d-1})$ is the daily log return for
firm~$i$ and $W_{i,t}^{[h]}$ is the post-filing window of length~$h$
calendar days for filing year~$t$. The two-day lag avoids the
same-day price reaction to the filing itself; the
$\sqrt{252}$ factor rescales the daily standard deviation to an
annualised figure, which is the conventional unit in the volatility
literature.

For the headline specification we use $h = 30$~days, which is short
enough to capture the post-filing reaction documented by
\textcite{loughran2011} but long enough that the volatility
estimate is built on a meaningful number of daily returns
($\approx 21$~trading days). Robustness checks at
$h \in \{5, 10, 90, 180, 365\}$ trace the temporal decay of the
signal (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_plan} and
Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness}).

\subsection{Financial controls}
\label{ssec:financial_controls}

The controls are taken directly from the firm's annual XBRL filings
(Section~\ref{sec:xbrl_tags}). Five regressors enter every
specification: \emph{lagged log-volatility}
$\ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}$ (the same measure as the dependent
variable but for the previous filing year's window of identical
length), \emph{firm size} $\ln(\text{Total Assets})$,
\emph{leverage} (total liabilities over total assets), \emph{return
on assets} (net income over total assets), and \emph{asset growth}
(year-on-year change in total assets divided by lagged total
assets). All ratios are winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentiles
within the regression sample to bound the influence of extreme
values without discarding economically meaningful observations.

\subsection{Text variables: Item~1A length and risk-word density}
\label{ssec:textual_features}

Two text variables operationalise the two channels of
Sections~\ref{sec:lit_volume}--\ref{sec:lit_specificity}.

\paragraph{Item~1A length (H1).} The volume channel is captured by
the natural logarithm of the cleaned word count of Item~1A:
\begin{equation}
\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t} \;=\; \text{number of tokens in Item~1A
of firm~$i$'s year-$t$ 10-K},
\end{equation}
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})$ enters the regression. The cleaning
pipeline (Section~\ref{ssec:text_postprocessing}) strips XBRL
tags, footnote markers, and table cell contents before tokenisation,
so the count reflects genuine narrative content rather than markup.
Across the sample the median Item~1A contains roughly $8{,}800$~words,
with a long right tail (Section~\ref{sec:descriptive}).

\paragraph{Item~1A risk-word density (H2) and its decomposition (H3).}
The specificity channel is captured by the share of words in
Item~1A that match the \textcite{loughran2011} uncertainty or
litigious word lists:
\begin{equation}
\text{RiskDensity}_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{
n^{\text{unc}}_{i,t} + n^{\text{lit}}_{i,t}
}{
\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t}
},
\label{eq:risk_density}
\end{equation}
where $n^{\text{unc}}$ and $n^{\text{lit}}$ are the per-section
counts of uncertainty- and litigious-list words. Higher values
indicate that a larger share of the section consists of generic
risk vocabulary; lower values indicate a section in which the
narrative is dominated by firm-specific or non-dictionary
content---names, numbers, geographies, products---that the
\textcite{hope2016} reading associates with greater specificity.

For H3 the same composite is decomposed into its two LM sub-lists,
each normalised by Item~1A length:
\begin{align}
\text{UncDensity}_{i,t} &\;=\; \frac{n^{\text{unc}}_{i,t}}
  {\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t}},
\label{eq:unc_density}\\[4pt]
\text{LitDensity}_{i,t} &\;=\; \frac{n^{\text{lit}}_{i,t}}
  {\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t}},
\label{eq:lit_density}
\end{align}
with the algebraic identity
$\text{RiskDensity} = \text{UncDensity} + \text{LitDensity}$. The
two sub-densities operationalise the conceptually distinct
uncertainty and litigious constructs of
Section~\ref{sec:lit_boilerplate}: $\text{UncDensity}$ captures
hedging vocabulary about what management does not know about its
own business, while $\text{LitDensity}$ captures the cautionary
legal-process boilerplate that legal advisers insert under the
safe-harbour regime of \textcite{nelson2007}.

\subsection{Why both variables are needed}
\label{ssec:why_both}

The two variables are mechanically related but not redundant. The
denominator of $\text{RiskDensity}$ is the same total word count
that enters H1 in logs. Across the sample the Pearson correlation
between $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ and $\text{RiskDensity}$ is
$-0.31$, which is far from collinear but is also not zero. Entering
the two variables jointly therefore lets each coefficient be
interpreted holding the other fixed: $\beta^{\text{len}}$ measures
the volatility response to a longer Item~1A at \emph{constant}
risk-word density (the volume channel net of any change in
boilerplate share), and $\beta^{\text{dens}}$ measures the
volatility response to a denser Item~1A at \emph{constant} length
(the specificity channel net of any change in volume).
Variance-inflation factors for both variables are below 1.5 in the
joint specification, well within conventional thresholds.

This joint specification of length and density is one of the
methodological points of the thesis. As discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:lit_specificity}, the dictionary-only risk measure
used in much prior work mixes the two channels into one statistic;
entering them jointly recovers two opposite-signed associations
that partially cancel when only the ratio is examined.

\section{Model specifications}
\label{sec:models}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Let $\mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}$ collect the four financial
controls (size, leverage, ROA, asset growth) and let
$\sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}$ denote the firm's lagged volatility computed on
the prior filing year's window of the same length~$h$.

\paragraph{Joint specification (H1 and H2).}
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \alpha
\;+\; \phi \, \ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}
\;+\; \boldsymbol{\beta}' \mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{len}} \cdot \ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})
\;+\; \beta^{\text{dens}} \cdot \text{RiskDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \mu_j \;+\; \tau_t
\;+\; \varepsilon_{i,t},
\label{eq:joint_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_j$ is a two-digit SIC industry fixed effect and $\tau_t$
is a fiscal-year fixed effect. Equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is
estimated at the headline horizon $h = 30$~days for the main table
(Section~\ref{sec:joint_results}) and at $h \in
\{5,10,30,90,180,365\}$ for the multi-horizon
table~\ref{tab:horizons}. H1 is supported if
$\beta^{\text{len}} > 0$ and significant at the 5\% level; H2 is
supported if $\beta^{\text{dens}} < 0$ and significant at the 5\%
level. Both predictions must hold jointly in
equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} for the volume--specificity
decomposition to be considered confirmed.

To document the marginal contribution of each text variable, the
results chapter also reports two partial specifications that drop
$\beta^{\text{dens}}$ (column~2 of the headline table) or
$\beta^{\text{len}}$ (column~3) from
equation~\ref{eq:joint_model}. The five-column nested table
(baseline / +length / +density / +both / +length+decomposition) makes
visible the fact that each variable carries meaningful incremental
information and that the composite RiskDensity hides a heterogeneous
mix of two LM sub-components, only one of which is active.

\paragraph{Decomposition specification (H3).}
For H3 the composite RiskDensity in equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is
replaced by its two LM sub-densities entered jointly:
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \alpha
\;+\; \phi \, \ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}
\;+\; \boldsymbol{\beta}' \mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{len}} \cdot \ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})
\;+\; \beta^{\text{unc}} \cdot \text{UncDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{lit}} \cdot \text{LitDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \mu_j \;+\; \tau_t
\;+\; \varepsilon_{i,t}.
\label{eq:decomposition_model}
\end{equation}
By the algebraic identity
$\text{RiskDensity}=\text{UncDensity}+\text{LitDensity}$, the
decomposition (equation~\ref{eq:decomposition_model}) nests the
joint specification (equation~\ref{eq:joint_model}) as the special
case $\beta^{\text{unc}}=\beta^{\text{lit}}$. Equation~
\ref{eq:decomposition_model} is estimated at the headline
$h=30$~days for column~(5) of the headline table
(Section~\ref{sec:joint_results}) and across all six post-filing
horizons in Table~\ref{tab:horizons}. H3 is supported if
$\beta^{\text{lit}} < 0$ at the 5\%~level with the sign and
significance preserved at every horizon, while $\beta^{\text{unc}}$
is small in absolute value and statistically indistinguishable from
zero. The Pearson correlation between UncDensity and LitDensity is
essentially zero ($r \approx -0.05$ in the regression sample), so
the two coefficients are separately identified.

\section{Estimation and inference}
\label{sec:estimation}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is estimated by ordinary least
squares with two-digit SIC industry and fiscal-year fixed effects
absorbed using the absorbing-regression routine of the
\texttt{linearmodels} package. Coefficients are therefore
identified from \emph{within-industry, within-year} variation,
which removes the risk that the results are driven by either time
trends in volatility (e.g.\ the 2008 and 2020 crises) or by
persistent industry-level differences in risk vocabulary or risk
levels.

\paragraph{Standard errors.}
Standard errors are two-way clustered by firm and fiscal year
following \textcite{petersen2009} and
\textcite{cameron2011robust}, which makes inference robust to
arbitrary within-firm correlation \emph{and} to arbitrary
within-year cross-sectional correlation in the residuals. Three
features of the data make the year dimension important alongside
the firm dimension. First, the dependent variable is itself a
volatility estimate computed on a rolling post-filing window, and
consecutive firm-year windows from a single firm overlap. This
induces strong serial correlation in $\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}$ that
ordinary heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors would
understate. Second, omitted firm-specific risk characteristics are
likely to persist over time. Third, calendar-year shocks (most
visibly the 2020 COVID shock) generate strong cross-sectional
correlation in residuals within a fiscal year that one-way firm
clustering would miss. Two-way clustering addresses all three
concerns simultaneously without imposing a particular correlation
structure.

\paragraph{Outliers.}
The financial ratios used as regressors are winsorised at the 1st
and 99th percentiles of the regression sample
(Section~\ref{ssec:financial_controls}). The text variables are
not winsorised: $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ is naturally bounded
because of the log transformation and $\text{RiskDensity}$ is a
ratio bounded between zero and one in practice (sample maximum
$\approx 0.05$).

\paragraph{Sample composition.}
After applying all data filters described in Chapter~\ref{ch:data}
and dropping firm-years with missing values for any regressor, the
estimation sample at the 30-day horizon consists of 5{,}691
firm-years on 535~unique firms. The 365-day horizon retains
6{,}172~firm-years on the same set of firms; the larger sample
reflects the fact that the longer window admits more observations
near the end of the panel period for which a 30-day window does
not yet have a complete 30~trading days of post-filing returns.

\section{Testing the hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypothesis_tests}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{H1 (Volume).}
Supported if $\hat{\beta}^{\text{len}} > 0$ at the 5\%~level in
equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} estimated at $h = 30$~days, and the
sign survives in the 365-day specification and at every intermediate
horizon. The economic magnitude is reported as the percentage
change in $\sigma_{i,t+1}^{[30]}$ implied by an interquartile-range
movement in $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$.

\paragraph{H2 (Specificity).}
Supported if $\hat{\beta}^{\text{dens}} < 0$ at the 5\%~level in
the same joint specification, with the sign and significance
preserved across horizons. The magnitude is reported analogously.

\paragraph{H3 (Boilerplate decomposition).}
Supported if, in equation~\ref{eq:decomposition_model} estimated
at $h = 30$~days, $\hat{\beta}^{\text{lit}} < 0$ at the
5\%~level and $\hat{\beta}^{\text{unc}}$ is statistically
indistinguishable from zero. The qualitative pattern
(LitDensity strongly negative, UncDensity null) must survive at
every horizon $h \in \{5, 10, 30, 90, 180, 365\}$ and on both
sub-period samples (Section~\ref{sec:robustness_plan}).

The three criteria are evaluated jointly. Statistical significance
alone is not enough: results that are statistically significant in
the headline 30-day specification but reverse sign at neighbouring
horizons would be evidence of overfitting rather than of a robust
economic channel.

\section{Planned robustness checks}
\label{sec:robustness_plan}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness} reports a battery of checks intended
to ensure that the headline results do not depend on a single
modelling choice. Briefly, equation~\ref{eq:decomposition_model} is
re-estimated
\begin{enumerate*}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item at five additional post-filing horizons (5, 10, 90, 180, and
        365~days) to confirm that the LM-Litigious sub-list is the
        active sub-component at every window;
  \item separately on the pre-COVID (2010--2019) and COVID-era
        (2020--2024) sub-periods, to confirm that the result is
        not driven by either macro regime;
  \item with firm fixed effects in place of industry fixed effects,
        which absorbs all time-invariant firm-level heterogeneity
        and isolates within-firm year-to-year variation.
\end{enumerate*}
Each check is reported in full in the tables of
Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness}.
