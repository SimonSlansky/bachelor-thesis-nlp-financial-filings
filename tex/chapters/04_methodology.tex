\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}
% =============================================================================

This chapter explains how the data described in
Chapter~\ref{ch:data} are used to test the three hypotheses
stated in the introduction. Section~\ref{sec:design} gives an
overview of the empirical strategy. Section~\ref{sec:variables}
defines the regression variables and Section~\ref{sec:models}
formalises the joint specification used for H1 and H2, the
LM-list-decomposition specification used as the second-stage test of
H2, and the
peripheral-silence specification used for H3.
Section~\ref{sec:estimation} describes estimation and inference.
The robustness battery is reported
in Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness}.


% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Empirical strategy}
\label{sec:design}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The thesis tests two competing predictions about the same section
of the 10-K---Item~1A, the mandatory risk-factor disclosure---in a
single regression specification. The volume channel
\parencite{campbell2014} predicts that longer risk-factor sections
indicate more risk and therefore higher post-filing volatility; the
specificity channel \parencite{hope2016} predicts that, holding
length constant, a higher share of generic risk vocabulary
indicates a more boilerplate, less informative section and is
associated with \emph{lower} post-filing volatility. Because the
dictionary risk-density commonly used in prior work has section
length in its denominator, separating the two channels requires
entering log word count and risk-word density jointly. As a
second stage, the composite RiskDensity is decomposed into the
LM uncertainty- and litigious-list densities to identify which
sub-list carries the H2 effect.

The dependent variable is the natural log of post-filing
annualised volatility, which symmetrises the right-skewed raw
distribution and makes coefficients interpretable as
semi-elasticities.

\section{Variables}
\label{sec:variables}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Dependent variable: post-filing log-volatility}
\label{ssec:dependent_variable}

The dependent variable is the natural logarithm of the annualised
standard deviation of daily log returns computed over a post-filing
window of length~$h$ that begins two trading days after the 10-K
filing date:
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \ln\!\left(
\sqrt{252}\cdot
\operatorname{sd}\!\bigl(r_{i,d}\bigr)_{d \in W_{i,t}^{[h]}}
\right),
\label{eq:vol}
\end{equation}
where $r_{i,d} = \ln(P_{i,d}/P_{i,d-1})$ is the daily log return for
firm~$i$ and $W_{i,t}^{[h]}$ is the post-filing window of length~$h$
calendar days for filing year~$t$. The two-day lag avoids the
same-day price reaction to the filing; the $\sqrt{252}$ factor
rescales the daily standard deviation to an annualised figure.
The headline horizon is $h = 30$~days; robustness checks at
$h \in \{5, 10, 90, 180, 365\}$ trace the temporal decay of the
signal (Chapter~\ref{ch:robustness}).

\subsection{Financial controls}
\label{ssec:financial_controls}

The controls are taken directly from the firm's annual XBRL filings
(Section~\ref{sec:xbrl_tags}). Five regressors enter every
specification: \emph{lagged log-volatility}
$\ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}$ (the same measure as the dependent
variable but for the previous filing year's window of identical
length), \emph{firm size} $\ln(\text{Total Assets})$,
\emph{leverage} (total liabilities over total assets), \emph{return
on assets} (net income over total assets), and \emph{asset growth}
(year-on-year change in total assets divided by lagged total
assets). All ratios are winsorised at the 1st and 99th percentiles
within the regression sample to bound the influence of extreme
values without discarding economically meaningful observations.

\subsection{Text variables: Item~1A length and risk-word density}
\label{ssec:textual_features}

Two text variables operationalise the volume and specificity
channels.

\paragraph{Item~1A length (H1).} The volume channel is captured by
the natural logarithm of the cleaned word count of Item~1A:
\begin{equation}
\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t} \;=\; \text{number of tokens in Item~1A
of firm~$i$'s year-$t$ 10-K},
\end{equation}
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})$ enters the regression. The cleaning
pipeline (Section~\ref{ssec:text_postprocessing}) strips XBRL
tags, footnote markers, and table cell contents before tokenisation,
so the count reflects genuine narrative content rather than markup.
Across the sample the median Item~1A contains roughly $8{,}800$~words,
with a long right tail (Section~\ref{sec:descriptive}).

\paragraph{Item~1A risk-word density (H2) and its LM-list decomposition.}
The specificity channel is captured by the share of words in
Item~1A that match the \textcite{loughran2011} uncertainty or
litigious word lists:
\begin{equation}
\text{RiskDensity}_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{
n^{\text{unc}}_{i,t} + n^{\text{lit}}_{i,t}
}{
\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t}
},
\label{eq:risk_density}
\end{equation}
where $n^{\text{unc}}$ and $n^{\text{lit}}$ are the per-section
counts of uncertainty- and litigious-list words. Higher values
indicate that a larger share of the section consists of generic
risk vocabulary; lower values indicate a section in which the
narrative is dominated by firm-specific or non-dictionary
content---names, numbers, geographies, products---that the
\textcite{hope2016} reading associates with greater specificity.

For the second-stage test of H2 the same composite is decomposed into its two LM sub-lists,
each normalised by Item~1A length:
\begin{align}
\text{UncDensity}_{i,t} &\;=\; \frac{n^{\text{unc}}_{i,t}}
  {\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t}},
\label{eq:unc_density}\\[4pt]
\text{LitDensity}_{i,t} &\;=\; \frac{n^{\text{lit}}_{i,t}}
  {\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t}},
\label{eq:lit_density}
\end{align}
with the algebraic identity
$\text{RiskDensity} = \text{UncDensity} + \text{LitDensity}$. The
two sub-densities operationalise the conceptually distinct
uncertainty and litigious constructs:
$\text{UncDensity}$ captures
hedging vocabulary about what management does not know about its
own business, while $\text{LitDensity}$ captures the cautionary
legal-process boilerplate that legal advisers insert under the
safe-harbour regime of \textcite{nelson2007}.

\subsection{Peer-relative omission gap (H3)}
\label{ssec:silence}

To test H3 we construct a peer-relative measure of strategic
silence. For each fiscal year $t$ we form a TF--IDF representation
of every Item~1A in the panel, restricting the vocabulary to terms
appearing in at least five filings overall (\texttt{min\_df}~$=5$)
and at most $95\%$ of filings (\texttt{max\_df}~$=0.95$); rows are
$\ell_2$-normalised. Let $\mathbf{x}_{i,t}$ denote the TF--IDF row
vector for firm~$i$ in year~$t$. For each two-digit SIC industry
$j$ in year~$t$ with at least six panel filings, we compute the
leave-one-out peer aggregate
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_{i,t} \;=\; \sum_{k \in S_{j(i),t}, k \neq i}
  \mathbf{x}_{k,t},
\label{eq:peer_aggregate}
\end{equation}
where $S_{j,t}$ is the set of panel firms in SIC2 industry $j$ that
filed in year~$t$. The peer aggregate $\mathbf{p}_{i,t}$ summarises
the topical content of what the focal firm's industry peers chose to
write that year, with the focal firm itself excluded. The
\emph{omission gap} is the share of peer disclosure mass on terms
the focal firm does not mention:
\begin{equation}
\textsc{Omission}^{1A}_{i,t} \;=\; 1 \;-\;
  \frac{\sum_{w : x_{i,t,w} > 0}\, p_{i,t,w}}
       {\sum_{w}\, p_{i,t,w}},
\label{eq:omission}
\end{equation}
where the sum in the numerator is over the vocabulary terms that
appear with non-zero weight in the focal firm's Item~1A. By
construction $\textsc{Omission}^{1A}_{i,t} \in [0,1]$. A value of
zero means the focal firm mentions every term any peer mentions;
a value close to one means the firm's own vocabulary covers a
small share of what its peers chose to discuss. Firm-years in
SIC2-year cells with fewer than five peers are dropped because the
peer aggregate is too noisy to support inference.

The measure has two properties that make it complementary to the
H1--H2 text variables. First, the peer aggregate
$\mathbf{p}_{i,t}$ is a function of \emph{other} firms' choices and
varies non-trivially within firm-year as the composition and
content of industry-year peers change over time. The within-firm
standard deviation of $\textsc{Omission}^{1A}$ is $0.054$ in the
regression sample, $46\%$ of the cross-sectional standard
deviation of $0.117$, so the firm-fixed-effects test of H3 has
real variance to exploit (in contrast to the LM sub-densities
used in the H2 decomposition,
which are largely absorbed by firm fixed effects;
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_firmfe}). Second, the measure is
mechanically negatively correlated with Item~1A length: a longer
Item~1A trivially mentions more peer-disclosed terms. The Pearson
correlation between $\textsc{Omission}^{1A}$ and
$\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ is $-0.85$ in the regression sample. The
H3 specification therefore enters $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ as a
control alongside $\textsc{Omission}^{1A}$, so that the
omission coefficient is interpreted holding length fixed. A
length-residualised version of the variable is reported as a
robustness check in Section~\ref{sec:robustness_silence}.

The peer-relative construction requires the SIC2 cell to be both
the relevant comparison set and a non-degenerate one. SIC2 is the
standard peer benchmark in the disclosure-similarity literature
\parencite{brown2011, dyer2017}; the cell-size median is 35~peers
(P25 = 14). Section~\ref{sec:robustness_silence} reports a
placebo specification in which SIC2 cell labels are randomly
permuted within fiscal year before recomputing the omission gap.
The H3 specification replaces \emph{asset growth} with two
liquidity proxies---the \emph{current ratio} and \emph{operating
cash flow over assets}---in the financial control vector. The
substitution follows the disclosure-cost logic of
\textcite{verrecchia1983,dye1985}: the incentive to withhold an
industry-relevant risk topic should be increasing in the firm's
proprietary or financing costs of disclosure, for which short-term
liquidity is the standard empirical proxy. Asset growth, which
captures the volume channel for H1 and H2, plays no analogous role
in the silence equilibrium.

\section{Model specifications}
\label{sec:models}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Let $\mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}$ collect the four financial
controls (size, leverage, ROA, asset growth) and let
$\sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}$ denote the firm's lagged volatility computed on
the prior filing year's window of the same length~$h$.

\paragraph{Joint specification (H1 and H2).}
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \alpha
\;+\; \phi \, \ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}
\;+\; \boldsymbol{\beta}' \mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{len}} \cdot \ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})
\;+\; \beta^{\text{dens}} \cdot \text{RiskDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \mu_j \;+\; \tau_t
\;+\; \varepsilon_{i,t},
\label{eq:joint_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_j$ is a two-digit SIC industry fixed effect and $\tau_t$
is a fiscal-year fixed effect. Equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is
estimated at the headline horizon $h = 30$~days for the main table
(Section~\ref{sec:joint_results}) and at $h \in
\{5,10,30,90,180,365\}$ for the multi-horizon
table~\ref{tab:horizons}. H1 is supported if
$\beta^{\text{len}} > 0$ and significant at the 5\% level; H2 is
supported if $\beta^{\text{dens}} < 0$ and significant at the 5\%
level. Both predictions must hold jointly in
equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} for the volume--specificity
decomposition to be considered confirmed.

\paragraph{LM-list decomposition specification (second-stage test of H2).}
For the second stage of H2 the composite RiskDensity in equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is
replaced by its two LM sub-densities entered jointly:
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \alpha
\;+\; \phi \, \ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}
\;+\; \boldsymbol{\beta}' \mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{len}} \cdot \ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})
\;+\; \beta^{\text{unc}} \cdot \text{UncDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{lit}} \cdot \text{LitDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \mu_j \;+\; \tau_t
\;+\; \varepsilon_{i,t}.
\label{eq:decomposition_model}
\end{equation}
By the algebraic identity
$\text{RiskDensity}=\text{UncDensity}+\text{LitDensity}$, the
decomposition (equation~\ref{eq:decomposition_model}) nests the
joint specification (equation~\ref{eq:joint_model}) as the special
case $\beta^{\text{unc}}=\beta^{\text{lit}}$. Equation~
\ref{eq:decomposition_model} is estimated at the headline
$h=30$~days for column~(5) of the headline table
(Section~\ref{sec:joint_results}) and across all six post-filing
horizons in Table~\ref{tab:horizons}. The H2 decomposition is supported if
$\beta^{\text{lit}} < 0$ at the 5\%~level with the sign and
significance preserved at every horizon, while $\beta^{\text{unc}}$
is small in absolute value and statistically indistinguishable from
zero. The Pearson correlation between UncDensity and LitDensity is
essentially zero ($r \approx -0.05$ in the regression sample), so
the two coefficients are separately identified.

\paragraph{Strategic-silence specification (H3).}
For H3 the joint specification of equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is
augmented with the peer-relative omission gap of
equation~\ref{eq:omission}:
\begin{equation}
\ln \sigma_{i,t+1}^{[h]} \;=\; \alpha
\;+\; \phi \, \ln \sigma_{i,t}^{[h]}
\;+\; \boldsymbol{\beta}' \mathbf{X}^{\text{fin}}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{len}} \cdot \ln(\text{Words}^{1A}_{i,t})
\;+\; \beta^{\text{dens}} \cdot \text{RiskDensity}_{i,t}
\;+\; \beta^{\text{om}} \cdot \textsc{Omission}^{1A}_{i,t}
\;+\; \mu_j \;+\; \tau_t
\;+\; \varepsilon_{i,t}.
\label{eq:silence_model}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:silence_model} is estimated under both fixed-effect
structures: the cross-sectional version with $\mu_j$ a SIC2
industry fixed effect (the headline H1+H2 specification) and the
strict version with $\mu_j$ replaced by a firm fixed effect
$\mu_i$, which absorbs all time-invariant firm-level
heterogeneity. The within-firm test is the more demanding of the
two because it isolates the year-to-year variation in omission
driven by changes in peer disclosure choices. Both specifications
are estimated at every horizon $h \in \{5, 10, 30, 90, 180\}$
days\footnote{The $h = 365$ day horizon is dropped from the H3
battery because the omission gap requires the firm-year to be
linked to a SIC2 cell of at least five peers in the same fiscal
year, and the post-filing 365-day window for fiscal years
$2023$--$2024$ extends past the data cut-off; the
$h \in \{5, 10, 30, 90, 180\}$ ladder retains the full overlap
with the H1+H2 horizon panel of Table~\ref{tab:horizons}.}
because the H3 prediction is about post-filing risk incorporation
over the same multi-week windows as H1 and H2.

The sample sizes for the H3 regressions are $4{,}630$ to
$4{,}636$~firm-years, smaller than the H1+H2 sample of
$5{,}691$ at the 30-day horizon because of the
$\geq 5$~peers-per-cell restriction needed to construct the peer
aggregate.

\section{Estimation and inference}
\label{sec:estimation}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Equation~\ref{eq:joint_model} is estimated by ordinary least
squares with two-digit SIC industry and fiscal-year fixed effects
absorbed using the absorbing-regression routine of the
\texttt{linearmodels} package. Coefficients are therefore
identified from \emph{within-industry, within-year} variation,
which removes the risk that the results are driven by either time
trends in volatility (e.g.\ the 2008 and 2020 crises) or by
persistent industry-level differences in risk vocabulary or risk
levels.

\paragraph{Standard errors.}
Standard errors are two-way clustered by firm and fiscal year
\parencite{petersen2009, cameron2011robust}. Two-way clustering
is necessary because (i)~consecutive firm-year volatility windows
mechanically overlap, inducing strong within-firm serial
correlation, and (ii)~calendar-year shocks (most visibly the 2020
COVID shock) generate strong within-year cross-sectional
correlation that one-way firm clustering would miss.

\paragraph{Outliers.}
The financial ratios are winsorised at the 1st and 99th
percentiles of the regression sample. The text variables are not
winsorised: $\ln(\text{Words}^{1A})$ is naturally bounded by the
log transformation and $\text{RiskDensity}$ is a ratio bounded
between zero and one in practice (sample maximum $\approx 0.05$).

\paragraph{Sample composition.}
The estimation sample at the 30-day horizon consists of 5{,}691
firm-years on 535~unique firms; the 365-day horizon retains
6{,}172~firm-years on the same set of firms.
