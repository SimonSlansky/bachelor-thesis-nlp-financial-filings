\section{XBRL tags}
\label{sec:tag_provenance}

Consolidated net income includes income attributable to non-controlling interests (NCI). The parent-only figure excludes it. The operating cash flow tags differ when discontinued operations have cash flows. For equity and for competing income measures such as continuing-operations income, the panel keeps the tag a firm reports most often. This uses later filings to choose a definition, while each value still comes from the filing for the year concerned.

Table~\ref{tab:tag_provenance} measures the size of these differences when a firm reports both figures. The net-income tags differ by more than 1\% of total assets in 303 of 3{,}838 firm-years. The two operating cash flow tags differ by that much in 50 of 417 firm-years.

\section{Parser implementation notes}
\label{sec:parser_impl}

The parser operates on the HTML DOM before flattening the document into text,
so that heading structure and placement survive the conversion. Candidate
headers are detected only when they match an ``Item X'' pattern, are short,
carry a heading cue, and are not hyperlinks. After filtering table-of-contents
entries and reference phrases, the parser keeps the candidate followed by the
longest section and then bounds the section by the next numbered item header.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize]
def find_candidates(node):
    for el in walk_dom(node):
        text = clean_text(el)
        if not re.search(r"\bItem\s+[1-9][A-C]?\b", text, re.I):
            continue
        if word_count(text) > 25:
            continue
        if not has_heading_cue(el):
            continue
        if has_hyperlink(el):
            continue
        yield el


def select_item(doc, item):
    candidates = []
    for cand in find_candidates(doc):
        nearby = nearby_text(cand, radius=300)
        if item == "1A" and "risk" in nearby.lower():
            candidates.append(cand)
        elif item == "7" and any(k in nearby.lower() for k in [
            "management", "discussion", "md&a"
        ]):
            candidates.append(cand)

    candidates = [
        c for c in candidates
        if not is_table_of_contents(c)
        and not is_reference_phrase_before(c)
    ]
    return choose_candidate_followed_by_longest_section(candidates)
\end{lstlisting}

When no candidate section of at least 200 words is found, the same logic is
applied to the referenced exhibit. In that case, numbered item headers are
replaced by title-based patterns such as ``Risk Factors'' or ``Management's
Discussion and Analysis'', because exhibits do not contain item numbers.

\paragraph{TF--IDF settings for the omission gap.}\label{par:tfidf_settings}
For each fiscal year, I fit the \texttt{scikit-learn} vectoriser on at least 50 earlier filings. It converts text to lower case, removes the library's English stop words and uses single tokens of at least two word characters (letters, digits or underscores). A token must appear in at least five training documents and in at most 95\% of them. The vectoriser uses raw word counts and smoothed inverse document frequencies, then scales each document vector to unit length.

\paragraph{H3 decision rule.} H3 predicts a positive omission coefficient. At the primary 30-day horizon, positive evidence requires a positive full-text estimate with Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ and a positive block-based estimate with unadjusted $p<0.05$. The adjustment covers five horizons (5, 10, 30, 90 and 180 days) under two fixed-effect structures \parencite{holm1979}. Negative evidence uses the same thresholds with opposite signs. Positive evidence under both structures supports H3, and positive evidence under one with no negative evidence under the other gives partial support. Negative evidence alone contradicts H3. All other outcomes leave H3 unsupported.
